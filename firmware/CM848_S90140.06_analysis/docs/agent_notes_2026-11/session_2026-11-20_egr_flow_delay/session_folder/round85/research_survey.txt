CM550 small stack argument survey (read-only; no repository file changed)

Answer: CM550's compiler does not pad a word argument to 4 bytes. It moves stack arguments in 2-byte units:
- Every 8-bit or 16-bit argument takes one 2-byte slot, written by move.w / clr.w or by a move.w store into the slot. The value sits at the next free offset.
- A byte value travels in a word slot, with the byte at the odd address.
- Longs and pointers take 4 bytes (move.l / pea / clr.l). They are not 4-aligned: they can start at +6, +10 or +14 after a word.
- No push in the walked code uses move.b to the stack.
Nothing in the ROM contradicts "word arguments are pushed as 2 bytes at their natural offset". The only thing that contradicts it is Ghidra's prototype model (4-byte slots, right-justified).

- starts from all 767 .cpp entries plus 10 call targets that have no body in the .cpp (777 in all);

4. WHAT LOOKS LIKE A CONTRADICTION BUT IS NOT
- Slot reuse: `548f` keeps 2 bytes of a previous pea slot for the next word argument, and move.w Dn,(sp) stores into a slot a deferred pop left behind. The word is still 2 bytes at its natural offset; it is written by a store, not a push.
- Pops are not reliable as argument sizes (deferred and merged pops).
- 0x32b38 takes the address of its +6 word argument (`486e 000a` pea (10,a6)) for byteSwap16. Its callers push two words (0x2ef6e `3f3c 0100`, `3f00`, `588f`).
- The CSV layer: every 1- or 2-byte CSV parameter gets a right-justified offset (+6, +7, +10, +11, +14) that the code never reads as that parameter. The exceptions are coincidences: 0xfaa6 at +7, 0x18968 at +14, 0x1cb76 at +6 (a real second word). 0x32244's `dtc_count` is a phantom: the body reads no stack argument and its only caller (0x2f5fc) pushes nothing.

7. RULE A FIX MUST MODEL (CM550 / this compiler)
- The first argument is at entry sp +4.
- Arguments are packed at 2-byte granularity in call order, with no alignment.
- 1-byte and 2-byte arguments take a 2-byte slot. Bytes sit at the odd address (+1 within the slot); a callee may still load the whole word.
- 4-byte arguments take 4 bytes at whatever even offset comes next.
- The caller cleans up, often late or merged, so pop sizes are not evidence of argument sizes.
